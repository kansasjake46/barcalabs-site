An export you can actually read

Most trackers treat export as an afterthought. Shotsy makes a PDF report. My TRT App offers a JSON backup. Regimen says you can export, but doesn’t say in what format.

Sovereign Vault’s export is built for what people really do with a record. They hand it to a doctor, or they paste it into an AI and ask questions. So everything comes out at once: every dose, what you took and what you missed, labs, training, weight, blood pressure and your watch data. It reads in order, and the numbers come with plain explanations.

There’s a short brief for a quick appointment and a full record you can paste anywhere. And there’s a spreadsheet with one row per day, plus a tab each for doses, labs, training, weight and vitals. Open it and your whole protocol is right there. A PDF is a picture of your data. This is your data.

It’s honest about itself, too. The on-protocol figure always shows its split: taken, late, skipped, not logged. Nobody reading it gets a rounded number.

Log without opening the app

A tracker only works if you log. Most reminders still make you unlock the phone, find the app, find the compound and tap four times. That’s where records die. It’s why so many people end up with two weeks of data and then a gap.

In Sovereign Vault, the reminder is the log button. Press and hold it, tap Log all, and you’re done. The app never opens.

The reminder shows how many doses are due, never what they are. A phone left face-up on a desk gives nothing away.

None of the other apps here say they can log from the reminder. Shotsy lists reminders and home-screen widgets. Regimen lists smart reminders. If that’s changed since 30 September 2026, tell us and we’ll update this.

Why so many of these are free

Every app costs money to build. If you’re not paying up front, the money comes from somewhere else: a subscription later, or your data.

You don’t have to guess which. Apple makes every developer declare what they collect. It’s on the App Store listing, under App Privacy. Most people scroll right past it.

Read the label, not the marketing. Every app here says it respects your privacy. Their labels don’t all agree.

That’s not an accusation. It’s just the difference between an app that collects your health data and one built so it can’t.

What the subscriptions cost

Three years of a $39.99 plan is $119.97. Three years of a $99.99 plan is $299.97. Three years of Sovereign Vault is $34.99. So is year ten.

A protocol record gets more valuable the longer you keep it. That’s exactly where a subscription works against you.

To be fair, subscriptions pay for ongoing development. An app you buy once has to earn its keep another way. Ours does it by keeping your record readable, and yours, for as long as you want it.

Questions people ask

Can I buy a dose tracker once instead of subscribing?

Yes. Sovereign Vault is $34.99 once, with no subscription and no account. Shotsy, Regimen, My TRT App and Glippy are all subscriptions. Checked 6 November 2026.

Why are GLP-1 and TRT tracker apps free?

Because they make money later, or from your data. Check the App Privacy section of any listing. It says what the developer collects and whether it’s linked to you. Regimen and Glippy declare health data linked to you. Shotsy declares health data collected for analytics, not linked to you. Sovereign Vault declares one identifier, for its update check: not linked to you, not used for tracking, and no health data at all.

What happens to my record if I stop paying?

In Sovereign Vault, nothing. Reading and exporting stay free forever. Paying only unlocks new entries. With a subscription app, check what happens to your history when a billing period ends.

Sovereign Vault does, training included, all on one timeline. If something isn’t in the library, you add it. Regimen covers compounds, labs and Apple Health data. Shotsy is GLP-1 only. My TRT App is built around testosterone.

Which dose tracker works without an account?

Sovereign Vault has no account and no sign-in. My TRT App requires an Apple or Google sign-in. Glippy says you need an account for cloud backup, but not to use it on your phone.

Can I get my data out?

Sovereign Vault exports your whole record as text you can paste anywhere, or as a spreadsheet. An encrypted backup moves everything to a new phone. Whatever app you pick, check its export before you log years of data in it.
